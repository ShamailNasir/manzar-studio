The models

Three ways to work with us, not a supermarket

Sprint

Who it suits
A defined problem you already understand: an audit, a prototype, a cost review, a proof of concept for one feature, a design pass on one flow.
What you get
A fixed two-week block, one senior team, a written scope agreed before day one, and a working output you can act on, not a slide deck.
What it doesn't include
Ongoing build, multi-feature scope, or anything that can't be described in one sentence before we start.
Typical range
From $6,000. Indicative. Confirmed after Discovery.

Build

Who it suits
A v1 with real scope: an AI product, an ERP or operations platform, a connected-device system, or a startup MVP that has to reach the market first.
What you get
A milestone-billed engagement, a working cut you can watch or click every Friday, and a fixed number once scope is locked in Discovery.
What it doesn't include
Undefined scope billed by the hour, an open-ended timeline, or work that starts before the number is agreed.
Typical range
$25,000 to $120,000, depending on scope. Timeline runs about six weeks for a startup MVP, three to five weeks for a marketing site, and eight to fourteen for a larger v1 platform. Indicative. Confirmed after Discovery.

Retainer

Who it suits
Teams who've shipped a v1 with us and want the same senior team on call: ongoing features, monitoring, incident response, or a production schedule that doesn't stop after one campaign.
What you get
Reserved monthly capacity with the people who built the thing, priority response, folding the ninety days of post-launch support already included in every Build into an ongoing rhythm.
What it doesn't include
A ticket queue, junior hands, or a contract that outlives the relationship. Cancel with notice.
Typical range
Priced monthly, scoped to reserved capacity. A starting figure isn't published yet. Qualitative only; see note above.

What changes the number

Scope depth, third-party integrations (payment rails, hardware vendors, systems we don't control), compliance or regulatory requirements, and timeline compression. Rushing a Build shortens Discovery, not the price.
